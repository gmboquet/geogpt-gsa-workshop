Why this question is current

Seismic enhancement increasingly aims to recover subtle structure, not only to sharpen displays. Yang, Dai and Ma’s recent study combines sparsity and smoothness to preserve weak reflectivity. Wang and colleagues explore a different route through multichannel spectral fitting. The research challenge is deciding which additional features are supported by evidence when the forward model is imperfect.

This exercise independently implements the first paper’s objective on public F3 log-derived simulations. It tests transfer to another interval and sensitivity to wavelet phase. It does not reproduce either paper’s private field experiments or introduce a new inversion algorithm.

Geological target
Acoustic-impedance contrasts mark changes in elastic properties. Weak coefficients may represent subtle interfaces; they are not automatically particular lithologies.
Research standard
Keep calibration separate from evaluation. Compare actual coefficient errors, weak-signal behavior, and false or missed peaks. Preserve failures as evidence.

11–17 MINUTES

3. Audit the geological evidence

Identify measured data, derived quantities and assumptions.

Inspect the seismic section and the density/sonic logs. Calculate one impedance row: V=1,000,000/DT for DT in μs/m; Z=ρV. Reflectivity is the impedance contrast divided by the impedance sum. Development and evaluation use different parts of the same log, with a gap. They are not independent wells. The observed F3 section supplies context; the test traces are controlled simulations from real log-derived reflectivity.

17–24 MINUTES

4. Select on development and freeze

Compare sparse, smooth and mixed objectives.

L1 favors a small number of nonzero coefficients. L2 stabilizes amplitudes and their vertical variation. Mixed regularization combines both. Its extra flexibility also creates more tuning choices. The prepared search selects each method by full-reflectivity normalized squared error on development only. Weak-coefficient error is reported separately; the minima may occur at different settings. Record which criterion you would use for your geological application before evaluating.

Request in GeoGPT Research

Run investigation.py calibrate unchanged. Show development-grid.csv, 02-calibration.png and the chosen settings. Explain the differences between L1, L2 and Mixed, the number of candidates, the selection criterion and solver convergence. Preserve frozen-settings.json. Do not inspect evaluation truth or change the objective to obtain a preferred winner.

Checkpoint: A frozen configuration and your prediction for its transfer to the withheld interval.

24–31 MINUTES

5. Test the withheld interval

Compare actual errors rather than visual sharpness.

The first test keeps the assumed and true phase equal and uses 20 dB signal-to-noise ratio. Twenty-four noise realizations share the same held-out geology. Each method sees the same realization. NRE is squared coefficient error divided by squared true coefficients; predicting zero everywhere gives NRE=1. A low data residual does not guarantee recovery of geological coefficients. Read weak and full errors together.

Request in GeoGPT Research

Run investigation.py evaluate --label matched. Show 03-evaluation.png, the summary and paired-comparison.csv. Explain one actual paired difference by subtracting the Mixed error from a baseline error. Compare both full and weak error against the zero-reflectivity reference of one. Report false and missed peaks using the frozen threshold and tolerance. Keep noise-realization variation distinct from population confidence. State what the data support without declaring a method universally best.

Checkpoint: An evidence-backed method comparison, one arithmetic check and an unresolved alternative.

31–37 MINUTES

6. Challenge the wavelet assumption

Change one assumption and retain the parent comparison.

Write what a phase error should change before running the challenge. Use a true phase of 30° while inversion continues to assume 0°; keep the calibration, interval, seeds, frequency and noise specification unchanged. This isolates one controlled form of mismatch. It is an exploratory challenge after the first test. A visually plausible reconstruction can still reflect the wrong pulse model.

8. Write and export the decision

Explain what you would do next as a geoscientist.

A publication-quality claim would additionally require independent wells or acquisition groups, broader sensitivity and an appropriate observation model.
