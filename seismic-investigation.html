Why this matters

A trace is one vertical column of the seismic image. Its colour shows signed amplitude, not rock type or water content. Two-way time includes travel down to the boundary and back up. Tuning means interference between nearby reflected pulses: they can reinforce or partly cancel one another. Seeing a signal and separating two rock boundaries are different tasks.

Why this matters

If two rocks have identical impedance, their boundary cannot reflect a pulse in this model. Impedance means density multiplied by wave speed. If the sand has zero thickness, its opposite top and base reflections coincide and cancel. These are checks on the programme, not proof that it describes a particular aquifer.

Sources and method boundaries

Widess, How thin is a thin bed? provides the foundational thin-bed problem. SEG: Thin beds, tuning, and AVO illustrates the wedge-model approach. This lesson uses only vertical reflection; angle-dependent AVO is outside its scope.

Professional application: Krzywiec, Słonka and Poprawa, Solid Earth, sections 3.3.2 and 4.3 use wedge modelling to constrain the seismic appearance of deep igneous intrusions in the Baltic Basin. Their study integrates real seismic data and geological constraints with a more complete wave model. Our sand-bed lesson teaches the underlying resolution question; it does not reproduce that study or make a new field discovery.

The calculation treats each vertical column independently. It omits lateral wave propagation, attenuation, multiples, transmission corrections, noise and angle dependence. All rock properties are hypothetical assumptions. A dashed model boundary is known input, not a recovered interface.
