Can we give this tiny shell a reliable name?

Geologists count shells in sediment to help reconstruct past environments. A wrong name can change those counts. You will examine X-ray slices through one shell and ask GeoGPT whether its visible inside structure supports a name—or whether more evidence is needed.

What you will learn

Compare a claim with an image, using a scientific reference to decide what features matter.

The computer task: one picture and one prompt.

The lesson supplies every setting. You do not have to invent a question, read a paper or write code.

Four words, explained here

Foraminifer
A single-celled organism that makes a shell. Many live in the sea.

CT section
One X-ray slice through the shell. This image shows six different slices.

Genus
A group of closely related species. Here, two genus names are candidates.

Unresolved
The image does not show enough detail to decide. This is a valid result.

Nummulites: the description specifies an enrolled spiral sheet, with or without internal cavities; the illustrated median and transverse sections help interpret that coiled construction. Marginopora: a disc-shaped test with cyclic chambers, chamberlets connected by annular canals, and different main and lateral compartments. A test is the shell; a chamberlet is a small chamber subdivision; annular means ring-shaped. Both can have broadly circular outlines: silhouette alone is not decisive. Distinguish visible architecture from inferred microstructure. These two genera are comparison candidates, not an exhaustive identification key. Sources: https://forams.treatise.geolex.org/displayInfo.php?genera=Nummulites and https://forams.treatise.geolex.org/displayInfo.php?genera=Marginopora . Understand the result, one point at a time

The Treatise is an illustrated scientific reference for invertebrate anatomy. Its Nummulites entry describes a coiled sheet; its Marginopora entry describes chambers arranged in cycles with smaller divisions and ring-shaped connections. Those criteria are already included in the prompt. You do not need to create a key or visit another website.

Find one named panel.

Look at that panel in the supplied image. Match one visible light or dark pattern to the corresponding row of the reply.

Ask what the pattern establishes.

A circular band is visible evidence. A tiny connection between chambers may not be visible. Do not turn “I cannot see it” into “it is absent”.

Read the recommendation.

A provisional name is tentative. A referral means the image cannot settle the question. Either can be defensible if the stated evidence supports it.

A tempting claim to check

“It looks round, so I know its name.” Both candidates can look round. The internal arrangement matters; a round outline alone does not decide.

Talk it through

Use your own result. Speak to a partner or think quietly; you do not have to type an answer.

  1. Point to one feature in the image that you can verify without trusting GeoGPT.
  2. Which part of the proposed anatomy is too small or unclear to confirm?
  3. Would you include a tentative name in a count, or put this specimen aside for another image? Explain your choice.
After discussing: read an example explanation

A defensible answer can remain unresolved: the visible banding does not necessarily resolve the fine connections needed for a confident identification. The published dataset labels this specimen Marginopora santoensis. Agreement with that label does not prove that every required feature was visible.
